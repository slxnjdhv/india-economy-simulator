Calibration report · 2026-11-18

How closely the model matches India, 2015–2024

The model’s settings were chosen so that its economy reproduces key features of Indian data: how high and how variable inflation is, how much food prices swing, unemployment, the repo rate, the rupee, imports and the informal share of jobs.

Method: simulated method of moments. Each candidate set of settings was run on 10 economies for 20 years each. The program then adjusted the settings to minimise the gap to the data. In total it tried 401 sets of settings, in 620 seconds.

Limits

  1. This is a calibration, not a full statistical estimation: ten years of annual data can’t pin down every setting precisely.
  2. Definitions differ. Model unemployment is matched to PLFS usual-status unemployment, and the model’s trade balance is compared with India’s current account, which also includes remittances that the model leaves out.
  3. The model has no trend growth, so only the size of swings in GDP growth is compared.
  4. Average inflation is too low. The model averages about 4%, India about 5%. In the model the RBI meets its 4% target on average, while India’s inflation ran above target, especially in 2020–22.
  5. Inflation persistence is now close but slightly low. India’s figure is 0.23; the model’s is about 0.12. With only ten years of data, India’s figure is uncertain by roughly ±0.3. The model’s persistence comes from two sources. Wage-setters who don’t trust the 4% target link pay to last year’s inflation. And oil, harvest and capital-flow shocks are long-lasting, as they are in reality. An earlier version swung the wrong way (−0.3), because its shocks faded within months and its RBI over-corrected.
  6. World inflation is set low (under 1%). It stands in for every reason the rupee drifts down by about 3% a year, such as capital flows, not only price differences.
  7. No current-account deficit. The model has no remittances or foreign investment, so its trade balances out over time, while India runs a deficit of about 1% of GDP.
  8. GDP swings are a little too large (about 2.0% against India’s 1.4%).
